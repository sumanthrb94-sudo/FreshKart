01The day

Everything else in this document hangs off this clock. An order taken on Monday is delivered Tuesday morning — the app never promises same-day.

06:30–07:30Shop shut
Office
Price update window

Back from the mandi, the admin enters today's rates on /admin/prices and publishes. Ordering is blocked for this hour even if the shop is otherwise open, so nobody buys at yesterday's price while the sheet is half-entered.

08:00Opens
Automatic
Shop opens · yesterday's van leaves

Two things share this hour. The catalogue goes live for today's buyers, and the van loaded overnight departs on yesterday's run. That is why 08:00 — not the 21:00 cart close — is the boundary that decides which run a newly assigned order joins.

08:00–09:00Delivering
Executive
Yesterday's orders reach the door

The executive works their stops in sequence, takes rejections at the door, and collects cash. The office closes the run when the van is back and the cash is counted.

08:00–21:00Trading
BuyerOffice
Today's orders come in

Buyers order for tomorrow morning. The office watches /admin/orders live, takes phone orders through /admin/pos, and can start assigning executives at any point in the day — an assignment is not something to save for the evening.

21:00Cart closes
Automatic
The day's orders are final

Checkout shuts. Nothing further can be added to tomorrow morning's van.

21:00–08:00Overnight
Office
Pick, pack, assign, load

Pull the consolidated pick list from /admin/reports → Packing, mark the batch PACKED, assign every order to an executive, print slips, load. Packing crosses midnight on purpose: an order assigned at 23:00 and one assigned at 01:00 are the same van.

Overriding the clock

/admin/settings can force the shop OPEN or CLOSED regardless of the hour — a holiday, no stock, a demo, a late run. An override expires automatically at the next 21:00, so a forgotten toggle cannot silently leave the shop trading for days.

If prices are not published

Buyers see "getting best live prices for you" instead of the catalogue. A publish counts as today's if it lands on today's IST calendar date — a 06:30 publish is valid, it does not have to be after 07:00. Yesterday's sheet never counts.

02System map

Three apps against one database. There is no application server in the request path — browsers talk to Firestore directly, and Firestore's security rules are the authorization layer.

Because there is no server in the middle, the rules carry weight the UI cannot. They check that an order belongs to the buyer creating it, that the delivery fee matches the slab, that payment starts UNPAID, that the total weight is within 500 kg, and that only an executive assigned to a stop can touch it.

03Who does what

Four roles exist in the data model — BUYER, ADMIN, DRIVER, SELLER. Three are in use.

Buyer

The shop owner ordering

  • Signs in with a mobile number and an OTP. No password ever.
  • Orders per kg against today's published rates.
  • Can cancel until an executive is assigned — after that the crates are on the van and they must call the office.
  • Inspects at the door and refuses what they don't want, before paying.
  • Downloads an invoice once delivered.
Office / Admin

Runs the day

  • Signs in at /admin-login with a username and password.
  • Publishes the daily price sheet.
  • Packs, assigns, prints slips, closes runs.
  • Decides the rejections too large for an executive to settle alone.
  • Takes phone orders on behalf of a customer through the POS screen.
Delivery executive

Works the stops

  • Signs in at /driver-login, same credential style as the office.
  • Sees only their own run — never anyone else's, by rule as well as by screen.
  • Records what the buyer refuses, with photos.
  • Collects cash at the adjusted figure.
  • Hands a stop back when a door cannot be worked.

05Order to door

One order, across the full 24 hours and all three roles.

06Office SOP

Four blocks a day. The morning one is the only one with a hard deadline — miss it and the shop cannot trade.

Morning  Publish the rates · by 08:00

  1. Sign in at /admin-login

    Username and password. The buyer app never links here.

  2. Open /admin/prices and enter today's rates

    Every active product. Yesterday's figures are shown for reference.

  3. Publish

    One tap. Until this happens, buyers see a waiting message instead of the catalogue — so if the shop looks empty at 08:15, this is the first thing to check.

  4. Check stock on /admin/products

    Anything you cannot supply today, set inactive or set its stock to zero. It is cheaper than cancelling the order tonight.

Through the day  Watch the board · 08:00–21:00

  1. Keep /admin/orders open

    It updates live and chimes on a new order. No refreshing.

  2. Take phone orders on /admin/pos

    Search the customer or create one, build the cart, choose cash or online. The order behaves exactly like one the buyer placed themselves.

  3. Answer support threads on /admin/support

    Anything a buyer escalates lands here and is flagged for a human.

  4. Clear rejection approvals immediately

    An escalated adjustment means an executive is standing in a shop, unable to collect, waiting on you. This queue leads /admin/returns for that reason.

  5. Assign executives as you go

    Assignment is allowed at any hour and does not need to wait for the evening. Anything assigned from 08:00 onwards joins tomorrow morning's run.

Overnight  Pack and load · 21:00–08:00

  1. Pull the pick list — /admin/reports → Packing

    One consolidated line per product across every order of the day. Pick against this, not against individual orders.

  2. Select the day's orders and mark them PACKED

    One bulk action from the orders screen.

  3. Assign every remaining order to an executive

    /admin/deliveries lists what is still unassigned. Anything left unassigned simply does not go out.

  4. Print the packing slips

    One slip per customer per address — multiple orders to the same shop merge into a single crate.

  5. Load in stop order

    The run screen sequences stops nearest-first from the depot, so loading in reverse of that order puts the first drop at the tailgate.

Next morning  Close the run · from 09:00

  1. Wait for the van

    Watch progress live on /admin/deliveries. The board shows stops done, cash expected and anything handed back.

  2. Count the cash against the run summary

    The figure shown is the adjusted total — rejections at the door are already deducted. Nothing to reconcile by hand.

  3. Press Close run

    This is a deliberate act and nothing does it automatically. It is the moment the cash is accounted for.

  4. Deal with what came back

    Closing releases every undelivered stop to the unassigned pool. Reassign it to today's van or cancel it — it will not ride along invisibly.

If a run is not closed

It stays on the board and is marked overdue in amber. That is intentional — an old run is never quietly folded into today's numbers. If yesterday's run is still showing, it has not been closed.

07Delivery executive SOP

One screen, one run, one direction. The executive never sees another executive's stops.

At the depot  08:00

  1. Sign in at /driver-login

    Username and password. The session persists — you should not have to sign in every morning.

  2. Check the run against the crates

    The screen lists every stop with its order numbers. Anything on the screen that is not on the van, or the reverse, is a question for the office before you leave.

On the road

  1. Work the stops in the order shown

    They are already sequenced nearest-first. Each stop has a Navigate button that opens maps, and a Call button for the shop.

  2. Hand over and let the buyer inspect

    This is the only moment ground truth exists. Do not leave before they have looked.

  3. Record anything refused, before money changes hands

    Enter the refused quantity per item and photograph it. The payable amount drops on the spot. See Money at the door.

  4. Collect and mark the stop delivered

    Take the adjusted amount for a cash order. Prepaid orders need no collection. Marking delivered records the payment at the same time.

  5. Hand a stop back if the door cannot be worked

    Shop shut, nobody there, wrong address — hand it back with the reason. It returns to the office's unassigned pool. Do not carry it to tomorrow.

Back at the depot

  1. Hand the cash in against the run summary

    The office has the expected figure on screen, already net of rejections.

  2. Flag anything delivered but unpaid

    The summary lists these separately — they are the ones to explain.

Never do this

Do not collect on an escalated rejection. If a refusal is over your limit the screen says so and holds the amount. Collecting the full figure and sorting it out later means money has to move twice, and the second move usually does not happen.

08Money at the door

There is no post-delivery return. Produce is inspected at handover and the bill drops before cash moves — so money changes hands exactly once, and there is never a refund to reverse.

What an executive can settle alone

The allowance is the greater of a flat floor and a share of the order. A flat cap alone would send every trivial rejection on a large order for approval; a percentage alone would leave small orders with almost no headroom.

₹2,000₹200₹500

Why the limit exists at all

A delivery run that stalls every time nobody answers the phone is worse than the occasional small write-off. Routine rejections settle at the door; only genuinely material ones wait for a decision.

Cash reconciliation

What an executive should be carrying is derived from the orders, never typed in and stored: the adjusted total of every delivered cash order on that run. Prepaid orders never pass through their hands and are listed separately, as is anything delivered but still showing unpaid. A counted-cash figure that can drift from the orders is worse than no figure, because it looks authoritative.

09The run

A run is one executive plus one delivery date — not "everything ever assigned to them". Without the date, a run finished on Friday still showed as in progress on Saturday and merged its totals into Saturday's first assignment.

How stops are sequenced

Orders going to the same customer at the same address merge into one visit — one crate, one door, one conversation. Visits are then ordered nearest-first from the depot by straight-line distance. It is a sensible loading and driving order, not a solved shortest route, and an executive who knows the area should trust their own judgement over it. The service area defaults to a 15 km radius plus an explicit list of served pincodes.

Staying signed in

A session survives closing the tab, reopening on another tab, following a deep link, and coming back days later. It ends on one thing only: signing out. Nobody should be asked for a mobile number twice.

Someone who verifies their number and then abandons sign-up before entering their details is a real state, not a broken one — they come back to the details step with their number already verified, and a way out if it was the wrong number.

Neither staff URL is linked from the buyer app. They are bookmarks.

11Open items

Where the software does not yet match the process described above. Stated plainly so nobody plans around a screen that is not doing what it appears to.

Out for delivery never lights up

SHIPPED exists and the buyer's tracker has a stage for it, but neither the overnight bulk action nor the executive's app ever sets it — orders jump from PACKED straight to DELIVERED. A buyer watching the tracker gets no signal that the van has left. Setting it when a run starts would close the gap.

Three of four report tabs show demo data

On /admin/reports, only Packing reads live orders. Inventory, Purchase and Invoices are wired to the demo seed dataset and will show invented figures in production. Do not order stock or reconcile against them.

Line prices are trusted from the browser

The security rules check the order's weight, its fee, its total derivation and its ownership, but not each line's unit price against the catalogue — that check was removed because it made orders past three products fail outright. Restoring it needs a small server-side route, which also needs the service-account credential added to the hosting environment.

Housekeeping still outstanding

Four service-account keys were shared during development and are still valid — revoke them in the Firebase console. The staff-provisioning route also needs its service-account environment variable set before new executives can be created from the admin screen.
